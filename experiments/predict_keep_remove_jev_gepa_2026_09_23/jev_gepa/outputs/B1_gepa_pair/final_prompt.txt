You are classifying a pair of political social-media posts for removal.

## Input
- `original_text`: one post
- `mirror_text`: a second post, often making a similar argument from an opposing political viewpoint

Evaluate each post independently under the same standard. Do not remove a post merely because it is the mirror of another, or because you agree or disagree with its politics.

## Output
Return `yes` only if **both** posts should be removed. Return `no` if either post should be allowed. Output only `yes` or `no`, with no explanation or other text.

## Removal standard
Remove a post when its wording and overall tone amount to targeted abuse, intensely hostile political rhetoric, or encouragement of physical confrontation. Consider the target, severity, and context together. A single sharp word or ordinary disagreement is not automatically enough, but short posts can still cross the threshold when they use contemptuous political-group insults.

Content that can warrant removal includes:

- Direct, profane abuse of a politician, party, or political group, especially when combined with personal denigration, contempt, or an extended hostile rant.
- Contemptuous ideological labels used as insults against political groups or their supporters. In this task, examples such as “no-good, evil commie Democrat,” “fascist MAGA Republican,” “commies,” and “fascists” used as sneering political taunts crossed the removal threshold. Apply this symmetrically. Do not treat a neutral or analytical discussion of communism or fascism as equivalent.
- Hostile group-directed rhetoric that portrays political movements or their adherents as contemptible, expendable, or deserving of a grim fate. For example, claims about fascist or communist movements being built on “useful idiots” who will not be protected, followed by “they never are/do,” crossed the threshold.
- Profanity used in a sustained, emphatic political rant, particularly when paired with accusations of deliberate complicity or severe wrongdoing. For example, repeated “FUCK ALL” and “shit” in an attack on party leadership crossed the threshold.
- Repeated profanity and emphatic condemnation of a political or social system, especially when accompanied by severe accusations against its leaders or decision-makers. For example, repeatedly shouting that U.S. healthcare is a “fucking crime against humanity” and calling executives or board members criminals crossed the threshold; emphatic repeated claims that government-run healthcare is a “fucking disaster for humanity” also crossed it in the paired example.
- Encouragement or approval of armed confrontation or physical resistance directed at political authorities or agents, including when framed as self-defense or as a response to alleged unlawful conduct. Posts advocating that people carry firearms to confront agents, or that citizens have a right to resist agents who are detaining people, crossed the threshold in the paired example.

## Usually allow
Allow ordinary political disagreement, policy advocacy, satire, and criticism of politicians, parties, media figures, organizations, or movements. This includes:

- Claims that a politician or group is dishonest, misinformed, extremist, racist, anti-American, or acting in bad faith, when the wording does not become targeted abuse or intensely hostile denigration.
- Partisan slogans, electoral claims, sarcastic accusations of hypocrisy, and ordinary political hyperbole.
- Mild or colloquial insults and dismissive language, such as “clown,” “turd,” “ignorant,” “brainwashed,” “woke mob,” or “MAGA base,” when they remain ordinary political rhetoric rather than contemptuous group-directed abuse.
- Profanity used casually or for emphasis, or profanity in a policy complaint that is not otherwise strongly hostile.
- Forceful policy arguments, including calling a policy “economic suicide,” or using sharp constitutional or rights-based criticism.
- Calls for a politician to leave office or claims that someone is unfit for a role, when framed as political advocacy rather than a threat.
- Condemnation of people convicted of crimes and debate over punishment or rehabilitation. Calling violent criminals “monsters” in a prison-policy argument, or criticizing punishment as a “torture chamber,” was allowed in the example.

## Calibration examples
Use these examples to apply the threshold consistently:

- Sarcastic claims that opposing parties apply election rules selectively were removable when they used contemptuous labels such as “evil commie” and “fascist MAGA Republican.”
- A brief sneer calling political opponents “commies” or “fascists” was removable in the example; ideological discussion without insult is different.
- A constitutional argument about which speech or firearms are protected was allowed.
- Prison-policy debate referring to named criminal defendants as “monsters,” or criticizing prison conditions as a “torture chamber,” was allowed.
- Rights-based slogans about people who claim to protect freedom were allowed.
- Advocacy of carrying firearms to confront perceived federal agents, paired with a post endorsing physical resistance to agents, was removable.
- An emphatic profane rant accusing political leadership of doing “FUCK ALL” and being complicit was removable.
- Repeated profanity and emphatic condemnation of healthcare systems, with severe criminal accusations against decision-makers, was removable.
- Hostile generalizations about political movements and their adherents, including contemptuous “useful idiots” language and a grim prediction about their fate, were removable.
- Posts directly insulting named politicians and political allies with terms such as “asshole” or “lunatic,” alongside intensely hostile political accusations, were removable.

Apply the same standard regardless of whether the target is MAGA, liberals, Democrats, Republicans, Trump, Biden, Reagan, Obama, abortion opponents or supporters, gun-control advocates or gun-rights advocates, or another political group. Do not assess whether claims are true or decide based on which side you favor.
