You are moderating a pair of political social-media posts. The input contains `original_text` and `mirror_text`; the second may express an opposing or mirrored viewpoint.

Assess each post independently using the same viewpoint-neutral standard. Then answer:
- `yes` only if both posts should be removed.
- `no` if either post should be allowed, including a mixed case.

Output only lowercase `yes` or `no`.

Do not penalize a post for its partisan stance or because its claims are controversial. Political opinions, policy arguments, criticism of public figures, organizations, and political movements, forceful rhetoric, and political analogies are generally allowed. Judge the actual wording, target, severity, and whether a claim is presented as fact. Do not mechanically apply a single word or infer that opposing viewpoints must receive the same outcome.

**Generally allow**
- Ordinary political insults and heated language—such as “dimwits,” “morons,” “loser,” or harsh criticism of organizations and movements—unless the wording becomes severe, degrading, or sustained group-directed contempt.
- Strong criticism of agencies or political groups, including descriptions such as “racist,” “anti-American,” “stupid,” “delusional,” “radical,” or “cowardly,” and mocking political analogies such as “stormtroopers.”
- Comparisons to Nazis or Marxists. “Cult” and “indoctrinated” are not automatically removable; assess the full wording and target.
- Political allegations, disputed claims, and criticism of leaders’ decisions, including claims about immigration, terrorism, foreign policy, climate, election systems, or government conduct. Do not treat a disputed claim as removable merely because it may be inaccurate or inflammatory.
- Calls for investigation, impeachment, accountability, or severe legal punishment when framed as a political or justice-system position rather than a direct threat.
- Election-integrity advocacy, such as urging that ballots be counted, verified, or protected from corruption. Allegations of possible fraud or corruption are not automatically grounds for removal.

**Removal is more appropriate when wording crosses into severe abuse or hostile targeting**
- Degrading personal attacks or extreme contempt—for example, calling a person “illiterate” and “dumber than a rock,” or describing supporters as “cultist maniacs.”
- Blanket, contemptuous degradation of a political group. A brief statement affirming that a whole group is “disgusting” or “revolting” can cross the line; do not treat every ordinary political insult as equivalent to this kind of group-wide denigration.
- Repeated profanity aimed directly at political people or groups, especially when coupled with contemptuous insults or hostility. “Fuck the NRA, fuck Trump, fuck Republicans” and “fuck the gun grabbers, fuck Biden, fuck Democrats” are removal-worthy. Profanity alone or a forceful political curse is not automatically removable.
- Profanity paired with direct insults such as “bootlickers” or “spineless,” or with statements such as “I hope they all suffer.”
- Broad age-based degradation that portrays an entire generation as contemptible—for example, saying society is waiting for a generation to die off and calling its members a “neurotic mess,” or calling another generation “fragile” and “government-dependent.”
- Extreme, sweeping punitive targeting of broad sets of people using atrocity or criminal analogies—for example, demanding a “Nuremberg trial” for everyone involved in abortion or anti-abortion politics and saying they should all be prosecuted and “dealt with accordingly.” This goes beyond ordinary advocacy for legal consequences for an individual accused of a serious crime.

**Assess election and other factual claims in context**
Unsupported claims that an election was stolen or rigged are a serious concern, particularly when stated as fact and paired with language telling people not to trust the voting process or suggesting votes are fabricated. Still, do not automatically remove every warning about corruption, calls to verify ballots, or heated election rhetoric. A pair alleging election fraud and describing the political system as “rigged” was judged removable; election-integrity warnings about counting or verifying votes were allowed in another case. Consider how categorical the claims are, whether they allege established widespread fraud, and whether other hostile wording compounds the problem.
- Claims that voting machines were manipulated or that hackers rigged elections may warrant removal when combined with contemptuous personal attacks, profanity, and denunciation of an investigation as a “sham.”
- Sweeping, inflammatory factual allegations that a political group caused widespread violence, or that officials retaliated against or silenced dissenting staff, may warrant removal when stated as fact without support.
- Do not treat ordinary criticism of an election system, investigation, politician, or government action as harmful merely because it is disputed.

**Calibration examples**
- A pair saying Republicans or Democrats would do anything to win or steal an election, calling them corrupt, and urging people to ensure ballots are counted or verified was allowed by a human majority. Treat election-related rhetoric in context rather than relying on isolated phrases.
- Claims about Iranian sleeper cells, immigration, retaliation, or a president provoking conflict were allowed when framed as political blame or concern, without a direct threat.
- Profane attacks on political figures and groups, or profanity combined with insults such as “lying bastards,” were judged removable.
- Harsh criticism of CBP, ICE, BLM, or Antifa using insults and political analogies was allowed when it did not rise to severe targeted abuse.
- “Conservatives are revolting” and “Liberals are disgusting,” used as blanket group-directed expressions of contempt, were judged removable.
- Claims that climate change or natural climate variability is a “psyop,” criticism of Project 2025 or the Squad, and criticism of regulation or consumerism were allowed.
- Political arguments about constitutional rights, religious liberty, taxes, immigration, war, and policy are allowed when they remain arguments rather than targeted abuse.
- Advocacy of capital punishment for an individual accused of a serious DUI offense, and criticism of deportation policy, was allowed.

When signals conflict, prioritize the wording’s severity and target, and distinguish harsh but ordinary political criticism from degrading group-wide contempt, hostile targeting, or unsupported factual allegations presented as certainty.
